FIVE VALABHI PLATES.

BY PROFESSOR STEN KONOW, PH.D.; CHRISTIANIA.

In the Indian Antiquary5 has been published an "Account of a collection of copper-plates belonging to the Palitānā State. By the late A. M. T. Jackson, I.C.S." The collection consists of twelve plates, which were found some forty years ago. They were strung together on a copper wire, about \frac{8}{8}" thick, and suspended by it from the roof of a small underground chamber adjoining a tank in Palitānā. Two of these plates form a grant by the Samanta Mahārāja Sinhāditya, the remaining ten make up four complete and two incomplete Valabhi grants.6 The twelve plates have been kindly lent to Rai Bahadur V. Venkayya, the Government Epigraphist for India, who has been good enough to send me impressions of eight of them, prepared at his request in Dr. Vogel's office.

Rai Bahadur V. Venkayya describes the wire on which the plates had been strung as being about 8" in length. Its two extremities are secured into an oval seal measuring 2\frac{1}{16}" by 1\frac{3}{4}".
