No. 28 : PLATE LXVI — CHIPŁŃ STONE INSCRIPTION OF MALLIKĀRJUNA : ŚAKA YEAR 1078

THE stone bearing this inscription was discovered under the wall of a house at ChipłŃ in the Ratnāgiri District in South KoŃkań in 1879, as stated in the proceedings of a meeting of the Bombay Branch of the Royal Asiatic Society held on the 3rd September 1879. It is very briefly noticed in the Bombay Gazetteer, Vol. I, part ii, p. 20, n. 1. Dr. Kielhorn calculated its date in the Indian Antiquary, Vol. XXIII, pp. 116-117, and included it in his List of the Inscriptions of Southern India, p. 56. It was edited for the first time with a facsimile by Dr. M. G. Dikshit in the Journal of the University of Bombay (New Series), Vol. XIII, part i, pp. 60 f., and was re-edited from the same facsimile by Dr. S. G. Tulpule in his Prāchīna Marāṭhī Kōriva Lēkha, pp. 53 f. It is edited here from the same facsimile. The stone is now deposited in the Prince of Wales Museum, Bombay.

The inscription is engraved on two faces of a stone, which originally must have measured about 1' 10" (55.88 cm.) by 1' 10" (55.88 cm.). It is referred to as Śāsana-stambha in line 12 of the present inscription and so may have formed part of a pillar.
