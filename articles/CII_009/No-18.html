No. 18 : PLATE XLV

— VIHĀR STONE INSCRIPTION OF ANANTADĒVA I : ŠAKA YEAR 1003

THIS inscription was first noticed in the Bombay Gazetter, Vol. XIV (1882), p. 379, where its date Šaka 1003 (A.D. 1081) was mentioned, and it was referred to the reign of the Śilāhāra king Anantadēva.3 It was subsequently noticed by Pandit Bhagvanlal Indraji in the Bombay Gazetter, Vol. I, part ii, pp. 18-19, f.n. 7. Bhagvanlal stated that the inscription mentioned the Chief Minister Rudrapai, Ajapālaiya, son of Mātaiya of the Viyādīka family, and the grant of some drammas to Kharāsān Maṇdali. It was supposed to contain a reference to the settlement of the Parsi community from Khōrāsān in Irāṇ4. The inscription was first edited without a facsimile by Dr. M. G. Dikshit in his Selected Inscriptions from Mahārāshītra (Marathi), pp. 69 f., and later, with a facsimile by Dr. S. G. Tulpule in his Prāchīna Marāṭhī Kōrīva Lēkha (Ancient Marathi Inscriptions), pp. 15 f. I edit it here from a fresh stampage.

The inscription is said to have been found at Vihār in the Thānā District, and is now deposited in the Prince of Wales Museum, Bombay. It is on a sandstone slab 4' (121.92 cm.) by 1' 5" (43.18 cm.), with a rounded top, on the right and left sides of which there are the sun and the moon. There are only four lines of somewhat defaced writing in the upper half and three more almost completely effaced in the lower half.
