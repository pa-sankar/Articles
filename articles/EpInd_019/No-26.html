THE PANCHADHARALA PILLAR INSCRIPTION OF THE EASTERN CHALUKYA KING VISVESVARA : SAKA-SAMVAT 1329.

BY J. NOBEL, PH.D., BERLIN UNIVERSITY.

Two inked estampages of this inscription were supplied to me by the late Mr. Venkayya through Prof. Lüders. The inscription is engraved on three faces of a pillar in the kalyāṇa-maṇdapa in the Dharmalingēsvara temple at Pañchadhārala in the Vizagapatam district. The name of the engraver is Dēvāchāri.

The characters are Telugu not differing from those of the pillar inscription of King Chōḍa III of the Kōṇa dynasty, dated in Śaka-Samvat 1325 which has been published above.3 The following peculiarities, however, may be noted. The remark about the combinations ttha, ddha, and stha does not hold good for the present inscription. In the former, da and dha, i and ī could clearly be distinguished, in the latter, they are constantly mixed up.

The language is Sanskrit verse throughout; only at the beginning and the end there is some prose. In the beginning we have Śrī-Viśvēsvarāya namaḥ, which is an invocation to Śiva, the illustrious lord of the universe. At the end comes the passage Hari-Hara-Hiraṇyagar-bha(ē)bhyō namaḥ which means 'adoration to Vishnu, Śiva and Brahma', and is followed by the name of the engraver Dēvāchāri, written in Telugu as Dēvāchāri-likhitamu. The style is very similar to that of the foregoing inscription, from which one verse (v. 25) has been borrowed almost verbally. This is not surprising if we bear in mind that the former inscription is but four years older than the present. There can be little doubt that both originated from the same author.
