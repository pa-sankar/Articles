The Morality of the Ramayana—A Rejoinder.

BY

MR. T. PONNAMBALAM PILLAI, M. R. A. S.

In preparing my paper on the Morality of Ramayana, to the “Malabar Quarterly Review” I intended to interpret the views of the late professor Sundaram Pillai, to a larger circle of readers than the Tamil article, in the Tamilian Antiquary, (No. 2), was able to command, in order to elicit rational criticism, in the interests of the Southern Indian History. As education advances it is but natural that investigations into the history of one’s own land, particularly if it is enveloped in mystery, should be made. At all events, such a spirit pervades at present the minds of all educated men both in Southern India and Ceylon, where most of the scenes in the Ramayana have been laid. If in the course of such a legitimate enquiry old ideas are exploded, all right thinking people ought to welcome such a healthy change, and sympathise with the workers in their difficult labours. In the place of such generous treatment, I least expected a furious, but a pointless attack, from the pen of Mr. Sadasiva Iyer, the talented Chief Justice of Travancore, who is known for his perfect equanimity of temper, and who until now never permitted it to be ruffled by anything, as became an arbiter of Justice in the Highest Court of the kingdom. I also regret, that in the heat of the fight he should have so forgotten himself, as to introduce into the arena of discussion, a large number of unparliamentary expressions (about 40 in number besides unlimited inuendoes), thus following the vernacular controversies he has condemned after taking exception to my using certain strong terms; and as a crowning act, that he should have suggested, that some evil spirit, probably from the nether world, impelled me to set my pen to the article in question.
