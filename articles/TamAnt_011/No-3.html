THE CANARESE WAR*

BY

T. PONNAMBALAM PILLAI ESQ., M.R.A.S.

IN spite of the fact that the Dravidians were the possessors of a civilization even more ancient than that of the Aryans, the history of that part of India occupied by them is still enshrouded in mystery. It is to be regretted that even the times within the memory of man are in that cimmerian darkness. Any attempt to remove this veil should be welcome.

While I had been making researches in the history of this ancient land, I came across a cultured young gentleman—a scion of an ancient family in South Travancore which gave a Prime Minister to that estate—who had been silently working towards the same end. He placed at my disposal a few manuscripts of popular songs, which certain classes of people in that part of the country sing in the months of November and December, to the accompaniment of a lyre in the form of a bow, which has consequently given the poems the name of Vil Adichan Pattu meaning songs of the beater of bow, and requested me to make out what I could with the aid of the scanty knowledge of Tamil I possessed.

The heading of the most important of the manuscripts is 'Cannadian Pêr' (കണ്ഠമുധന്ത പോറി) which means the War of the Canarese. The verses are evidently in heroic metre and I do not think they are to be found committed to writing. The copies before me were taken down as they were recited.
