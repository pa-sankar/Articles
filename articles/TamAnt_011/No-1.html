THE CHOLAS AND THEIR COINAGE

BY

DIWAN BAHADUR T. DESIKACHARI, B.A., B.L., M.R.A.S.

CENTURIES before the Christian era, the Cholas had established themselves on the coast of Coromandel (Chola mandalam) and enjoyed a civilization not less advanced than that of the Aryan Settlements in the North. They were referred to along with the Keralas and the Pandyas in the Second Rock Edict of the Buddhist Emperor, Asoka, in the third century B.C. The great chronicle of Ceylon, an ancient work in Pali called the Mahavanso, made it the theme of one of its main episodes, the war waged against the Island by the Chola Tamils in the early centuries preceding the Christian era.

Some scholars have advanced the hypothesis that it was a colony of Dravidian merchants who, becoming acquainted with an alphabetic writing derived from the white Pre-Semetic Akkadians north of the Euphrates Valley, brought the script to India, being thus the first to introduce the art of writing into Dravida Desam. There is in the proof that is adduced in support of this hypothesis much that tends to show that the Tamils, as early as the seventh century before the Christian era, had developed such a degree of maritime and commercial enterprise as to tempt them to undertake voyages across the sea to distant countries. The principal port of the Cholas was Kaveripaddinam, situated near the northern mouth of the Kaveri.
