THE ORIGIN OF THE WORD 'ARYA'

BY

V. J. TAMBY PILLAI ESQ., M.R.A.S. (Ceylon)

THE name 'Aryas' () by which the Vedic race called itself in times of pre-historic antiquity seems to furnish a very important and interesting point of contact between that race and the primitive Dravidians. It is held to be a purely Sanscrit term derived from a primordial root akin to, or identical with, the Latin and Greek ar meaning 'to plough' and, hence, it has been inferred that, at first, it meant 'a cultivator', and then 'a lord' or 'a nobleman', which latter meaning it is said to retain till today. It may, however, be pointed out here that that eminent Sanscritist, the late Dr. K. M. Bannerjea of Calcutta, did not concur in the above explanation. He theorized that the word was originally of Semitic extraction but adopted into, and naturalized in, Sanscrit in later times. If the true history of the term and of its connexion with Sanscrit be that given by the learned Dr. Bannerjea, we need no further proof to show that the true 'Aryas' of antiquity were a race, not the same as that from which the primitive Sanscritians sprang. It is possibly a historical fact that the country beyond the Western frontier of the Punjab known as 'Ariana',*

The name Ariyan appears thus to be of Semitic origin, but adopted and naturalized in Sanscrit.
