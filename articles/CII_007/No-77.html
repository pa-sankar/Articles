RATANPUR STONE INSCRIPTION OF JAJALLADEVA I : (KALACHURI) YEAR 866

THIS inscription is incised on the beautifully polished surface of a red sand-stone which was found at Ratanpur, 16 miles north of Bilaspur, the headquarters of the Bilaspur District in Madhya Pradesh. It is now deposited in the Central Museum, Nagpur. The record was edited, with a translation and a lithograph, by Dr. Kielhorn in the Epigraphia Indica, Vol. I, pp. 33 ff. It is edited here from the original stone and the lithograph accompanying Dr. Kielhorn's article.

"The inscription consists of 31 lines. The writing originally covered a space of about 2' 21" broad by 2' 2" high. At present, a small portion of the upper proper left corner and a large portion of the lower right corner of the stone are broken away, so that from 4 to 9 aksharas are missing at the end of the first five lines and from 2 to about 25 aksharas at the beginning of the last thirteen lines.' Small portions of the stone have also been broken away at the upper right and lower left corners, which has resulted in the loss or mutilation of about a dozen aksharas. Further, some aksharas which were clearly legible in Dr. Kielhorn's time, have since been broken away from the proper right edge of the stone.3 I have, however, succeeded in reading a few aksharas from their traces on the stone4 which Dr. Kielhorn found illegible from the impression supplied to him.

The characters are Nāgari. The size of the letters is about .5". The prishthamātrās are used in some places. The sign of avagraha occurs in ll. 12, 18, 24 and 29. The language is Sanskrit, and except for the introductory obeisance to Siva and the date at the end, the inscription is metrically composed throughout. There are 34 verses, all of which are numbered.
