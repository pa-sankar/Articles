How were the volumes processed?

Subscribe

How the printed volumes became the searchable articles on this site. AI marks a step done by an AI model, Machine one done by a script following fixed rules.

Please verify before you quote. These articles were read from the scanned volumes by machine (OCR) and put back together page by page. Mistakes may remain, most of all in Devanagari and other Indic script, and in the small footnote numbers. Please check the printed volume before using the text.

  1. Finding the scans

    Each volume was found on archive.org. Where several copies exist, the highest-resolution complete one was picked; a page missing from it is read from another copy and the article says so. The Sources page links every scan.

  2. Profiling every page Machine

    A script measures each page: text, plate, blank or cover; one or two columns; its resolution. Plates stay page images from here on, never read as text.

  3. Cutting columns Machine

    A two-column page is cut along its gutter, band by band, so the reading order (left column before right) is fixed before any text is read.

  4. Reading the text AI

    Pages are read by Surya, an open-source machine-learning OCR model, on a rented GPU. It keeps diacritics (ā, ṭ, ś) that older OCR drops. A few CII pages were read by Google's Gemini instead, where Surya had no reading.

  5. Checking the reading AI

    Claude (Anthropic's AI model) reads a sample of pages against the scans for each volume and flags dropped lines and misread characters. A page that fails is read again; a recurring misreading (an ! for ḷ at a word's end) is corrected by rule and logged for review.

  6. Putting the articles together Machine

    The volume's own table of contents is the master list: number, title, author, first page. Each article runs from its heading to the next one; running heads are dropped; footnotes are attached to the page they print on, numbered by their marks in the text. Every page keeps its printed number and its PDF page.

  7. Plates and figures Machine

    Plates are kept as images of the whole page, each on its own tab. Drawings inside the text are cut out and shown where they print. Web copies are compressed.

  8. Categories Machine

    The Inscriptions site's term lists (hero-stones, Bhairava, Rāmānuja and others) are matched against each article, the same way as for the inscriptions.

  9. Dynasty, king, date, place, language, script AI

    Gemini reads each article's introduction, the part where the editor states these facts in plain English, and returns each value with the sentence it came from. A value whose sentence is not in the article is rejected. The century is computed from the date, not asked. These values carry the AI badge on the site.

  10. Mysore Archaeological Reports Machine

    Each year's report is one article, by the Director who signed it. Its sections (Part, district, village, monument) come from the report's own contents and are found in the text by their headings and printed pages.
