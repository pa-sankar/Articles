17 reports from 1930, 1931, 1932, 1933, 1934, 1935, 1936, 1937, 1938, 1939, 1940, 1941, 1942, 1943, 1944, 1945, 1946 · 320 plates.

The Mysore Archaeological Reports are the yearly reports of the Archaeological Department of Mysore State: the year's tours, monuments, numismatics, manuscripts and the inscriptions found. Each report is one article, by the Director who signed it, opened at the section a search finds.

Please verify before you quote. These articles were read from the scanned volumes by machine (OCR) and put back together page by page. Mistakes may remain, most of all in Devanagari and other Indic script, and in the small footnote numbers. Please check the printed volume before using the text.
Searching. The search box looks through the full text of every article, footnotes included, and its citation. Accents and capitals are optional: kanchi finds Kāñchī. Choose how the term must match: anywhere in a word, at the start of a word (pallava finds Pallavas but not Rāshṭrapallava), or the whole word only. Each result shows how often the term occurs and a snippet around the first match.
The filter lists (Volume, Author, Dynasty, King, Century, Place, Language, Script) each include or exclude the values ticked, and narrow each other; the line under them says what is applied. A filter on Dynasty, King, Century, Place, Language or Script adds that column to the table. Click a column heading to sort by it.
Click a row for everything about the article: its pages, plates and images, and the dynasty, king, date, place, language and script picked out by AI from the introduction AI (tab Extracted from article). Each value was checked against the editor's own words before it was kept.
Reading an article. An article reads as one continuous text, as in the volume: a badge at the bottom right shows the printed page and the PDF page you are on, and jumps to a page. Footnotes are numbered through the article and gathered at its end (click a number to read one in place); plates stand at the end of the paragraph they interrupt, and all together at the end. Opened from a search, every match is highlighted and next / previous step through them. The Plates and Images numbers in the table open the first one.
Numbers match Roman numerals too: 18 finds XVIII.
